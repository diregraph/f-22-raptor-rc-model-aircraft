Sealing

Each bearing has a 2 mm lip on the downstream part that turns inside a counterbore in the upstream part, so the gap between the flanges sits behind a step, out of the jet. A 0.25 mm PTFE washer between the flange faces closes that gap and cuts the friction the motors fight; a second one behind the upstream flange takes up the play. Washers are shown white. Cut them from sheet with the DXF files in cad/dxf.

How it works

Bearing 1 is square to the duct. Bearings 2 and 3 are canted 40° in opposite directions. Turning 2 and 3 by equal and opposite angles bends the jet; bearing 1 turns the whole bend so it points straight down, and a little more or less to swing the jet sideways for yaw. Each bearing has its own drive, as on proven 3BSM models: the collar is a ring gear keyed to the part downstream, and a small N20 gearmotor with an encoder on the part upstream turns it through a 14-tooth pinion. At power-up the nozzle board drives each joint gently onto a stop (a lug on the collar meets the motor plate), then counts from there.

Before you run it

A printed fan at 18,000 rpm can shed a blade. Run it behind a board, wear eye protection, and never stand in line with the fan disc.
